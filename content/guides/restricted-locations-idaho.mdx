## Navigating Restricted Spaces and Special Conditions

While Idaho law is broadly permissive regarding the carrying of firearms, it is not without limits. The state has established specific locations where carrying a firearm is prohibited or restricted. Furthermore, certain privileges, such as carrying on a university campus, are reserved for individuals who have met higher training standards. Understanding these boundaries is a critical component of responsible and lawful gun ownership in Idaho.

### Prohibited Locations

Idaho Code § 18-3302C establishes the primary list of "gun-free zones" where carrying a concealed weapon is prohibited, even for those with a license or carrying under the state's permitless carry statute. These locations are:

*   A courthouse.
*   A juvenile detention facility or adult correctional facility and jail.
*   A public or private elementary or secondary school (K-12).

It is important to note that these state-level restrictions are complemented by other regulations. Private property owners and business entities retain the right to prohibit firearms on their premises. If a property is clearly posted with "no guns" signage, carrying a firearm onto that property could result in trespassing charges. Additionally, federal law prohibits the carrying of firearms in federal facilities, such as post offices, Social Security administration buildings, and the sterile areas of airports.

### Campus Carry

Idaho is one of a select few states with a specific law permitting concealed carry on the campuses of public colleges and universities. However, this right is not universal and is a prime example of how the state incentivizes advanced training. The ability to carry a concealed firearm on campus is a privilege granted *only* to individuals who hold an Idaho Enhanced Concealed Weapons License (ECWL) or are qualified retired law enforcement officers.

This right does not extend to those carrying under the permitless carry provision or with a standard CWL. The legislature has effectively created a system where a significant privilege—carrying in a location often deemed sensitive—is reserved exclusively for those who have voluntarily undergone the state-approved 8-hour training course required for the ECWL. This policy allows the state to expand carry rights while simultaneously arguing that it is promoting enhanced safety and legal knowledge for those who choose to carry in such an environment. The law does contain some location-specific exceptions within campuses; carrying is still prohibited in dormitories and in public entertainment or sporting facilities with a seating capacity of 1,000 or more.

### Carrying Under the Influence

A universal and critical restriction under Idaho law is the prohibition against carrying a concealed weapon while intoxicated. It is illegal to carry a concealed firearm while under the influence of alcohol or other intoxicating drugs. This law applies to all individuals, regardless of whether they hold a permit or are carrying under the permitless carry statute. This is a public safety measure that underscores the responsibility that accompanies the right to carry a firearm.
