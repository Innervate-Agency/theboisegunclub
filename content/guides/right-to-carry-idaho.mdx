## The Right to Carry in Idaho: A Multi-Layered System

Idaho's laws governing the carrying of firearms are among the most permissive in the nation. The state employs a multi-layered system that provides a baseline of broad freedom through permitless carry while offering optional, tiered licenses for citizens who seek specific privileges, such as reciprocity with other states or the ability to carry on a university campus. This structure is a direct and calculated response to the national landscape of concealed carry laws, designed to maximize in-state liberty while providing tools for navigating more restrictive jurisdictions.

### Permitless ("Constitutional") Carry

The foundation of Idaho's carry laws is "permitless" or "constitutional" carry. As of July 1, 2020, any U.S. citizen or current member of the U.S. Armed Forces who is at least 18 years old and not otherwise disqualified from possessing a firearm may lawfully carry a concealed weapon anywhere in the state without a license or permit. This right is not limited to handguns; it extends to other deadly weapons such as dirks, bowie knives, and daggers.

This statute represents a significant expansion of Second Amendment rights within the state, removing the requirement for law-abiding citizens to seek government permission to carry a concealed firearm for self-defense. In parallel, open carry of a firearm is also legal in Idaho for any person 18 years of age or older who can legally possess a firearm, and no permit is required for this practice.

### The Standard Concealed Weapons License (CWL)

Despite the legality of permitless carry, Idaho has retained its system for issuing a standard Concealed Weapons License (CWL). While no longer necessary for carrying a firearm within Idaho's borders, the standard CWL remains a relevant and valuable credential for several reasons. The license is issued by the local county sheriff to qualified applicants who are at least 18 years of age. The application process requires the submission of fingerprints for a state and national background check and a fee, which is approximately $56.10 for an initial application. The license is valid for five years.

The modern utility of the standard CWL is twofold. First, it serves as a basis for reciprocity with other states that recognize Idaho permits but do not have permitless carry themselves. This allows Idaho residents to legally carry concealed when traveling to certain other jurisdictions. Second, and of significant practical benefit within Idaho, possession of a valid Idaho CWL (either standard or enhanced) exempts the holder from the mandatory National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS) check when purchasing a firearm from a Federal Firearms Licensee (FFL). This can streamline the purchasing process for the license holder.

### The Enhanced Concealed Weapons License (ECWL)

In 2013, Idaho introduced the Enhanced Concealed Weapons License (ECWL) to address the growing complexity of interstate reciprocity. Many states require specific training standards to recognize another state's permit, and the ECWL was explicitly designed to meet and exceed these criteria. The requirements for obtaining an ECWL are significantly more stringent than for the standard license. An applicant must be at least 21 years old, though a restricted-term license is available for those aged 18-20. The applicant must also have been a legal resident of Idaho for at least six consecutive months or hold a current concealed carry permit from their state of residence if they are a non-resident.

The central requirement of the ECWL is the successful completion of a qualifying eight-hour handgun course within the 12 months preceding the application. This is not a basic safety course; the curriculum is mandated by statute to include specific components:

*   **Instruction on Idaho Firearms Law:** This portion must cover statutes relating to firearms and the use of deadly force and must be delivered by an active or retired law enforcement officer or a member of the Idaho State Bar.
*   **Firearm Safety and Use:** The course must cover the basic concepts of the safe and responsible use of handguns and principles of self-defense.
*   **Live-Fire Training:** The applicant must fire a minimum of 98 rounds during the course.

The ECWL is not merely an "advanced" permit; it is a tool of legal diplomacy. It was created to provide Idahoans with a credential that would be recognized by a much broader array of states, including those with stricter training requirements. Furthermore, the ECWL grants a significant privilege within Idaho that no other form of carry allows: the legal right to carry a concealed firearm on public college and university campuses.

### Transporting Firearms in a Vehicle

Idaho's laws regarding the transportation of firearms in a vehicle are a direct extension of its permissive carry philosophy. Idaho Code § 18-3302(4)(c) contains a critical exception to concealed carry restrictions, stating that the prohibition on carrying concealed without a license does not apply to "any deadly weapon concealed in a motor vehicle".

This provision means that any person who is legally allowed to possess a firearm can carry that firearm loaded and concealed within their vehicle without needing any type of permit. The firearm can be in a glove compartment, under a seat, or in a center console. This stands in stark contrast to the laws of many other states, which often require firearms to be unloaded, locked in a case, and stored in an inaccessible area like the trunk. While some sources suggest that non-residents may face different requirements, the primary state statute does not make this distinction, making Idaho one of the most straightforward states for in-vehicle firearm transport. Openly carrying a firearm in a vehicle is also permitted.
