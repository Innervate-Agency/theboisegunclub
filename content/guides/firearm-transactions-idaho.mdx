## Firearm Transactions and Ownership in Idaho

The legal landscape governing the purchase, sale, and possession of firearms in Idaho reflects the state's overarching commitment to minimal regulation and the protection of owner privacy. The state imposes few barriers to the acquisition of firearms, does not require registration, and has recently enacted forward-looking legislation to protect the financial privacy of gun buyers.

### Acquiring and Selling Firearms

In Idaho, the process of buying and selling firearms is remarkably straightforward, particularly between private citizens. State law does not require a permit to purchase a rifle, shotgun, or handgun, nor does it mandate any form of firearm registration.

The most significant feature of Idaho's transaction laws is the absence of a universal background check requirement. While federally licensed dealers (FFLs) are required by federal law to conduct a NICS background check for every sale, Idaho law does not require such a check for private sales between two individuals. This means that two Idaho residents who are not prohibited from owning firearms can legally buy and sell firearms from one another without the involvement of an FFL or any government paperwork.

However, it is critical to note that this freedom is not absolute. Federal law still makes it a crime to knowingly sell or transfer a firearm to a person who is legally prohibited from possessing one (e.g., a convicted felon, a fugitive from justice, or an unlawful user of controlled substances). This places a significant legal responsibility on the private seller to take reasonable steps to ensure they are not transferring a weapon to a prohibited individual.

### Age-Related Regulations

Idaho law establishes clear and distinct rules regarding the possession and sale of firearms based on age. These regulations differentiate significantly between handguns and long guns (rifles and shotguns).

**Possession:**

*   **Handguns:** Idaho Code § 18-3302F makes it unlawful for any person under the age of 18 to possess a handgun. This is a strict prohibition.
*   **Long Guns and Other Weapons:** Under Idaho Code § 18-3302E, a minor under 18 may possess a long gun or other weapon (excluding handguns) if they either have written permission from a parent or guardian or are accompanied by a parent or guardian.
*   **Minors Under 12:** Any minor under the age of 12 in possession of any weapon must be accompanied by an adult.

**Sales and Transfers:**

*   **To Persons Under 18:** It is a misdemeanor under Idaho Code § 18-3302A to sell a pistol, revolver, or gun to anyone under 18 without the written consent of the minor's parent or guardian.
*   **To Persons Under 16:** Idaho Code § 18-3308 makes it a misdemeanor to sell or give firearms or most types of ammunition to anyone under 16 without written parental consent. An exception is made for shotgun shells and.22 caliber rifle ammunition.

These laws create a legal framework that allows for the supervised introduction of young people to long gun sports and hunting while strictly controlling their access to handguns.

### Prohibited Persons

Idaho law, in conjunction with federal statutes, defines specific categories of individuals who are prohibited from purchasing or possessing firearms. Under Idaho Code § 18-3316, any person who has been convicted of a felony is prohibited from owning or possessing any firearm. The state's criteria for disqualification from obtaining a concealed weapons license, found in Idaho Code § 18-3302, provide a comprehensive list of other prohibited categories, which include individuals who:

*   Are fugitives from justice.
*   Are unlawful users of, or are addicted to, controlled substances.
*   Have been dishonorably discharged from the armed forces.
*   Are subject to a qualifying protection order.
*   Have been adjudicated as mentally ill or lacking mental capacity.

### The Second Amendment Financial Privacy Act

Reflecting an evolving legislative focus on modern threats to gun rights, Idaho passed the Second Amendment Financial Privacy Act in 2023. This law, codified in Idaho Code § 18-3326 and § 18-3326A, was a direct response to efforts by major payment card networks to create a specific Merchant Category Code (MCC) for firearms retailers. The legislature found that such a code could be used to facilitate "unprecedented surveillance of second amendment activity" and would have a "significant chilling effect" on the lawful purchase of firearms and ammunition.

The act makes it illegal for financial institutions to use a "firearms code" to surveil, report, or otherwise discourage constitutionally protected firearm and ammunition purchases. This statute demonstrates a proactive approach to protecting the transactional privacy of gun owners. It moves beyond traditional gun control debates to address the potential for data aggregation and financial surveillance to create a de facto registry of gun owners, marking a sophisticated and forward-looking development in the state's defense of the Second Amendment.
